# English oral tactics for Chinese-first speakers

The Yau finals are in English. The gap between polished written English and spoken English is a
red flag judges look for (ch.7) — close it by practicing aloud.

## Preparation
- **Rehearse aloud, don't memorize a script.** A recited talk sounds rehearsed and breaks under
  the first off-script question. Know the ideas; speak them fresh each time.
- **Drill your technical terms in English** until fluent — the words you'll say 50 times
  (your method, your metric, your key concept). Stumbling on your own core terms signals the
  work isn't internalized.
- Record yourself; check for the paper-vs-speech gap.

## During the defense
- **Define jargon on first use** — judges may be from an adjacent field.
- **Pace and pause** — slower is clearer; a pause to think reads as thoughtful, not as a gap.
- **"I don't know, but I'd test it by…"** — never a flat "I don't know," never a bluff.
- **Restate a misheard question** before answering: "Are you asking about {X}?"
- **Don't blame your mentor** for a choice — own your decisions.
- **Short, structured answers** beat long rambles: claim → evidence → (optional) caveat.

## Handling the hardest moment
- Whiteboard request (math/physics): walk up and do it. Hesitation reads as "not mine."
- A question that exposes a real gap: acknowledge it honestly, state how you'd address it. Maturity
  scores; defensiveness doesn't.

## Mindset
丘成桐: "中国学生素质完全可以比得上哈佛的学生。" The panel wants to see a young researcher who
owns their work — not a flawless performer. Clarity and honesty beat polish.
