# Finals presentation structure (~10–12 min)

A research-paper talk, not a poster walk. Slides support speech; you must be able to present
without them (ch.7 self-check #3).

## Recommended arc
1. **Title + one-line question** (15s) — the problem you posed (自己出题).
2. **Motivation / why this matters** (1 min) — the gap; social significance if applicable.
3. **Your one original idea, foregrounded** (1 min) — say explicitly "the contribution that is
   mine is …". Don't bury it.
4. **Method** (2–3 min) — enough to be credible and defensible; justify design choices.
5. **Results, with real numbers** (3 min) — quantified; figures with error bars; baselines/
   controls/identification visible.
6. **Limitations** (1 min) — honest scope; shows maturity, pre-empts "did you consider…".
7. **Contribution + future work** (1 min) — restate what's yours.

## Slide critique checklist
- [ ] The original idea appears as an explicit slide, not implied.
- [ ] Every results claim is quantified and traceable to a method step.
- [ ] No slide makes a claim the student can't defend at a whiteboard.
- [ ] Jargon is defined on first use.
- [ ] Math: key lemma/derivation has a backup whiteboard plan.
- [ ] CS: a slide shows datasets (provenance), baselines, ablations.
- [ ] Econ: a slide states the identification strategy + key assumption.
- [ ] Bio: a slide covers data provenance + ethics + validation.
- [ ] AI use is acknowledged where substantive (link to /yau-ai-compliance).
- [ ] Text is sparse; the student speaks, the slide reminds.
