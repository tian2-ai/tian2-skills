# The Yau mentor boundary — student leads, mentor assists

From 白皮书 ch.1 (理念). This is what makes a Yau mentorship different from a paid project shop —
and what protects the student at the all-English defense.

## The principle
"学生在这个比赛过程中应该主导整个科研课题的进展，老师更多的是发挥辅助性的作用，凭借老师多年的
经验和专业知识帮助学生学会并掌握科研的精神和流程。" The student leads; the mentor is auxiliary.

ch.1 explicitly warns against the failure mode where students ask the teacher to supply "课题、
课题实现过程，甚至结果乃至论文" — that is "极其违背比赛初衷的."

## A good mentor DOES
- Suggest directions and give feedback on the student's ideas.
- Teach research method and rigor.
- Provide access to equipment, lab space, literature.
- Challenge the student's reasoning (rehearsal for the panel).
- Sign as 指导老师 where required, having genuinely supervised (not authored).

## A good mentor does NOT
- Choose the topic for the student.
- Design the method or run the analysis instead of the student.
- Write the paper or the abstract.
- Hand over a "result" for the student to dress up.

## Why this matters at defense
The finals panel probes whether the work is the student's (ch.7). A student fronting a mentor's
(or AI's) work cannot answer "what is yours here," cannot re-derive the math, cannot explain the
mechanism. The mentorship boundary isn't just ethics — it's the difference between a defensible
paper and one that collapses under questioning.

## Coaching line for the student
"Use your mentor to learn how to do research and to pressure-test YOUR ideas. The moment the
topic, the method, or the paper becomes theirs instead of yours, you've lost the thing the award
is actually measuring — and you'll feel it at the whiteboard."
