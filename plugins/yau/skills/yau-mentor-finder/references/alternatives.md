# Alternatives when no faculty replies

Cold faculty email is low-yield. Realistic fallbacks, in rough order of accessibility:

1. **Your school science/math teacher.** Often the best first mentor and, where required, the
   formal 指导老师. Many Yau teams are school-mentored. They know you and are reachable.
2. **A teacher's network.** Ask your teacher for a warm introduction — this multiplies cold-email
   response rates many-fold.
3. **University outreach / summer programs.** Some universities run HS research programs; public
   and applyable. Check the institutions active in your subject (the winners archive lists which
   universities recur as advisors' affiliations).
4. **Graduate students / postdocs.** Often more responsive than senior faculty, and closer to
   hands-on technique. Find them via the lab's public page.
5. **Online research mentorship programs.** Some are paid (can be costly) — mentionable but
   outside this skill's scope; verify they respect the Yau "student leads" ethic.
6. **Peer + self-study.** For math especially, a strong self-directed student plus literature can
   go far — the award explicitly values 自己出题自己做.

Timeline reality: 4–8 weeks from search to a first conversation; longer to a working relationship.
Start early; don't let mentor-hunting delay the research itself.
