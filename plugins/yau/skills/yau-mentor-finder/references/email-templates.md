# Outreach email templates (drafts only — the student sends)

Personalize every slot. Send from a school email if possible. One follow-up max after 2 weeks.

## Template (cold, to faculty)
```
Subject: High-school student inquiry about [specific topic] research mentorship

Dear Professor [Last name],

I'm [Full name], a [grade] student at [School]. I'm preparing a research paper for the S.T. Yau
High School Science Award (丘成桐中学科学奖) in [subject], on [specific question].

I read your [paper / project] on [specific work], and [one concrete thing that interested the
student about it — shows you actually read it].

So far I have [one sentence on progress / what the student has done independently]. I'd be very
grateful for [a SMALL ask — e.g. "20 minutes to hear whether my approach is on the right track"].
I understand you're busy, and no reply is a fine reply.

Thank you for your time.
[Full name] · [School] · [school email]
```

## Rules — the draft MUST
- Have a specific, non-clickbait subject line (never "URGENT" / "Question for you").
- Identify the student in sentence one.
- Give ONE specific reason for THIS person (a real paper they read).
- Make a SMALL ask (a short chat), not "be my mentor."
- Acknowledge their time; full signature.

## Rules — the draft MUST NOT
- Flatter ("I've read all your work").
- Make vague asks ("any advice appreciated").
- Promise the impossible ("I'll work full-time on this").
- Attach files on the first email.
- CC multiple mentors (each email is individual).

## Honest expectations
10 well-targeted cold emails → typically 1–3 replies, 0–1 willing to mentor. A warm intro
(teacher/family knows them) multiplies the rate. Tell the student this so they don't despair.
