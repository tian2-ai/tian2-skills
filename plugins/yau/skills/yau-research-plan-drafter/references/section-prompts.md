# Section intake prompts (per subject)

Ask one section at a time; capture the student's OWN words. If they can't answer, that section
isn't ready — pause and design it, don't fabricate.

## Universal opener
- "In one sentence: what question are you answering, and what is the ONE idea here that is yours?"
  (the originality gate — feeds every subject's critique)

## Math
- "State your main result formally. What exactly are you claiming?"
- "What prior theorem(s) do you build on? Can you cite them precisely?"
- "Walk me through the key step of the proof — could you reproduce it without notes?"
- "What special case or example checks your result?"
- "What does your result leave open?"

## Experimental (phys/chem/bio)
- "What's your hypothesis, and what observation would falsify it?"
- "Describe your apparatus/materials and your procedure step by step."
- "What are your controls? Independent vs dependent variables? Sample size, and why that size?"
- "Any human participants, animals, tissue, hazardous materials? (ethics/risk)"
- "Where does your data come from, and how confident are you it's clean?" (bio panels drill this)

## Theoretical / numerical
- "State your model and ALL its assumptions."
- "Derive the key relation, or describe the simulation setup."
- "How do you validate — limiting cases, conservation laws, known results?"
- "Where does the model break down?"

## CS
- "State your contributions as a bulleted list. Which is genuinely new vs. using existing tools?"
- "Datasets: source, size, license, how labels were obtained?"
- "Baselines: name a naive one, a strong classic one, a recent SOTA. Why these?"
- "What ablations isolate the effect of your contribution?"
- "Explain WHY your method works — mechanism, not 'the model learned it'. Failure modes?"

## Econ
- "State your causal question precisely."
- "Data source, sample, construction, summary stats?"
- "Identification strategy — DID/IV/RDD/RCT/structural? What's the key identifying assumption?"
- "What robustness checks rule out alternative explanations?"
- "Where could correlation be masquerading as causation in your design?"
