# STS Rubric — the 4 criteria, sub-criteria, focus areas, and 1.0–5.0 anchors

> Verbatim from 「STS 四维评分标准（rubric.json）」. Do not invent or paraphrase
> the anchors beyond what is here. The score is a **SUM, not a maximum** — weakness on any one of
> the four criteria caps the composite.

## The shape of the score

- **4 criteria**, each scored `1.0–5.0` in **0.5 increments**, **equally weighted (25% each)**.
- **Composite = the SUM of the four**, out of **20.0**.
- **Typical composite: 9–12.** Most applications fall here.
- **Top-15% threshold ≈ 16.0** (roughly 4+ per criterion) — about 300–400 of 2,000+ applications.
- **Calibration exemplars:** High = **19.33** (exceptional across all four), Medium = **~12.5**
  (solid across criteria), Low = **~6** (weak in multiple criteria).
- Because it is a sum, a 5/5/5/2 = 17 is fragile and a single hole (the "2") is exactly what caps
  it. Balanced strength across all four beats a spike-plus-hole profile.

## Criterion 1 — Entry Form (/5, weight 0.25)

*Evaluation of academic achievement, recommendations, and activities/interests.* This is driven by
the transcript, the recommendations (Tasks 2a–c), and the activities list (Task 8). Depth on the
application structure → `/sts-application-navigator`.

### 1a. Academic Achievement
Focus: transcript quality (grades, course rigor) · test scores (SAT/ACT/AP/IB, if provided) ·
academic honors & recognitions · course selection demonstrating challenge.
- **5.0** — Exceptional academic record with consistently high grades in rigorous courses,
  outstanding test scores.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong academic performance with challenging coursework and solid test scores.
- **3.0–3.5** — Good academic standing with adequate rigor.
- **2.0–2.5** — Average academic performance with limited rigor.
- **1.0–1.5** — Below average academic achievement.

### 1b. Recommendations
Focus: quality/depth of educator recommendations · specificity of examples · insight into
character, work ethic, potential · project advisor/mentor recommendations.
- **5.0** — Exceptional recommendations with specific, compelling evidence of abilities and potential.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong recommendations with good examples and positive assessments.
- **3.0–3.5** — Adequate recommendations with standard praise.
- **2.0–2.5** — Generic or lukewarm recommendations.
- **1.0–1.5** — Weak or concerning recommendations.

### 1c. Activities and Interests
Focus: breadth & depth of extracurriculars · leadership roles & sustained commitment · science
competitions & awards · community engagement/service · passion beyond academics.
- **5.0** — Exceptional breadth and depth with significant leadership, sustained multi-year
  commitments, national-level achievements.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong involvement with leadership roles and notable achievements.
- **3.0–3.5** — Good participation with some commitment.
- **2.0–2.5** — Limited or shallow participation.
- **1.0–1.5** — Minimal extracurricular engagement.

## Criterion 2 — Scientific Merit (/5, weight 0.25)

*Evaluation of the scientific quality, methodology, and advancement of the research project.* Driven
by the Research Report (Task 5). Depth → `/sts-research-report-coach` and `/sts-data-analysis-tutor`.

### 2a. Scientific Validity
Focus: sound scientific principles & methodology · advancement of field knowledge · significance of
the research question · contribution to scientific understanding · proper terminology/concepts.
- **5.0** — Groundbreaking research addressing a significant question with potential for major impact.
- **4.0–4.5** — Solid scientific work advancing field knowledge with a clear contribution.
- **3.0–3.5** — Valid scientific approach with modest contribution.
- **2.0–2.5** — Basic scientific work with limited advancement.
- **1.0–1.5** — Flawed methodology or minimal scientific value.

### 2b. Scientific Setup
Focus: clear hypothesis/research question · appropriate experimental design · proper controls &
variables · suitable methodology · adequate sample size & statistical power.
- **5.0** — Exemplary design with sophisticated methodology and excellent controls.
- **4.0–4.5** — Well-designed study with appropriate methods and controls.
- **3.0–3.5** — Adequate design with basic controls and methodology.
- **2.0–2.5** — Weak design with limited controls or inappropriate methods.
- **1.0–1.5** — Poor experimental setup with fundamental flaws.

### 2c. Scientific Analysis
Focus: appropriate analysis techniques · recognition/discussion of error sources · valid
interpretation · logical conclusions supported by data · acknowledgment of limitations · future
directions.
- **5.0** — Sophisticated analysis with excellent recognition of limitations and well-supported conclusions.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong analysis with good interpretation and acknowledgment of errors.
- **3.0–3.5** — Adequate analysis with reasonable conclusions.
- **2.0–2.5** — Limited analysis or overreaching conclusions.
- **1.0–1.5** — Poor analysis or invalid conclusions.

## Criterion 3 — Student Contribution (/5, weight 0.25)

*Evaluation of the student's independent work, initiative, originality, and personal contribution.*
Driven by Task 4's six 200-word contribution boxes + the independence statement. Depth →
`/sts-contribution-coach`. STS is INDIVIDUAL — this criterion rewards clearly-attributed, original
student work and honest mentor boundaries.

### 3a. Student Independence
Focus: clear differentiation between student work and mentor contributions · the student's role in
each research phase · level of autonomy · evidence of independent problem-solving.
- **5.0** — Exceptional independence with the student driving the majority of the research process.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong independence with clear student ownership of key components.
- **3.0–3.5** — Moderate independence with adequate student contribution.
- **2.0–2.5** — Limited independence with heavy mentor involvement.
- **1.0–1.5** — Minimal student contribution or unclear differentiation from mentor work.

### 3b. Student Initiative
Focus: resourcefulness in problem-solving · seeking out opportunities/resources · proactive approach
to challenges · performance relative to available resources · self-directed learning.
- **5.0** — Exceptional resourcefulness and initiative, maximizing limited resources creatively.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong initiative with proactive problem-solving.
- **3.0–3.5** — Adequate initiative with some self-direction.
- **2.0–2.5** — Limited initiative, primarily reactive.
- **1.0–1.5** — Minimal initiative or resourcefulness.

### 3c. Student Originality / Creativity
Focus: unique approaches / novel solutions · creative designs or procedures · innovative use of
materials/techniques · original problem formulation · novel applications of existing methods.
- **5.0** — Highly original work with innovative approaches or novel discoveries.
- **4.0–4.5** — Creative approaches with clear originality in methods or thinking.
- **3.0–3.5** — Some originality with modest creative elements.
- **2.0–2.5** — Limited originality, mostly standard approaches.
- **1.0–1.5** — Minimal creativity or originality.

### 3d. Student Insight
Focus: learning from failures/setbacks · deep understanding of research context · vision for future
directions · scientific integrity & honesty · reflection on process and outcomes.
- **5.0** — Exceptional insight with deep reflection, learning from failures, and clear vision.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong insight and understanding with good reflection.
- **3.0–3.5** — Adequate insight with basic understanding.
- **2.0–2.5** — Limited insight or superficial understanding.
- **1.0–1.5** — Minimal insight or reflection.

## Criterion 4 — Overall Scientific Potential (/5, weight 0.25)

*Holistic evaluation of the student's potential as a future STEM leader and scientist.* Driven by the
essays (Task 7) and the whole picture. Depth → `/sts-essay-coach`.

### 4a. Student's Scientific Ability
Focus: analytical & critical thinking · work habits & discipline · mastery of scientific
concepts/methods · communication of scientific ideas · technical skills & competencies.
- **5.0** — Exceptional ability with advanced analytical skills and excellent work quality.
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong abilities with good analytical thinking.
- **3.0–3.5** — Adequate abilities with solid fundamentals.
- **2.0–2.5** — Developing abilities with room for growth.
- **1.0–1.5** — Limited scientific abilities.

### 4b. Student's Promise as a Scientist
Focus: long-term dedication to STEM · potential for future leadership · trajectory of development ·
passion & commitment · likelihood of a research career · potential for breakthrough contributions.
- **5.0** — Exceptional promise as a future STEM leader (Nobel Prize / Fields Medal / MacArthur
  Fellow potential).
- **4.0–4.5** — Strong promise with a clear trajectory toward significant contributions.
- **3.0–3.5** — Good promise with a solid foundation for a STEM career.
- **2.0–2.5** — Moderate promise with uncertain long-term commitment.
- **1.0–1.5** — Limited promise as a future scientist.

## Cross-cutting guidance evaluators apply across all four criteria

- **Holistic review** — evaluate the whole student, not just the best research; consider potential
  and trajectory.
- **Bias awareness** — be conscious of unconscious bias re gender, race, socioeconomic background,
  school resources, and research-topic familiarity.
- **AI-content detection** — flag suspected AI-generated content in essays/reports (generic
  language, lack of personal voice, inconsistent style). The application is **still scored** even
  when flagged; flagging feeds the integrity screen, not a zeroed score.
- **Resource context** — judge achievement relative to available opportunity; under-resourced
  students may show exceptional initiative (this is why 3b explicitly rewards
  performance-relative-to-resources).
- **Evidence-based scoring** — every score must be supported by specific evidence from the
  application. No evidence, no points.

## Compliance / disqualification items (run alongside scoring)

Evaluators note these even while still scoring the application; they feed the downstream integrity
and eligibility screens. Forms/approvals depth → `/sts-rules-wizard`.

- **Required approvals:** IRB for human subjects · IACUC for animal research · risk assessments for
  hazardous materials · proper citations in the research paper.
- **Prohibited / DQ-risk:** team projects with other high-school students · AI-generated essays or
  research reports (must be flagged) · research paper exceeding 20 pages (excluding title,
  abstract, bibliography) · plagiarism or uncited content.
- **Mandatory sections:** the six 200-word contribution descriptions · research limitations (200w) ·
  AI-usage disclosure (100w) · independence statement (200w).
