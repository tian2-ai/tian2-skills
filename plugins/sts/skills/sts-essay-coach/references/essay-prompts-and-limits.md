# Task 7 — the four essays, official 2026 prompts, limits, and per-essay checklists

Reference for `sts-essay-coach`. The essays feed the **Overall Scientific Potential** criterion
(communication, promise) in `rubric.json` and contribute to the Entry Form's writing-quality
read. They are scored — but the report and the Task 4 contribution boxes are where Scientific
Merit and Student Contribution live; keep this skill in its lane and route those out.

## Essay 1 — Research Project "Layperson's Summary" (200 words)

**Prompt intent.** Summarize your research project so a non-specialist understands it — while
staying scientifically accurate. Must cover four beats: **background, procedures, conclusions,
relevance.** (See `layperson-communication.md` for the hook + accuracy craft.)

Checklist:
- [ ] Opens with a HOOK a journalist could quote (not "My project investigates…").
- [ ] Background: the question in one plain sentence a smart 12-year-old gets.
- [ ] Procedures: what you actually did, in lay terms (no method-name soup).
- [ ] Conclusions: what you found — with the SAME number used in the paper and other essays.
- [ ] Relevance: why someone outside the field cares.
- [ ] Accurate under simplification — no analogy that smuggles in a false claim.
- [ ] ≤ 200 words.

## Essay 2 — Project Benefits & Impact (200 words)

**Prompt intent.** What are the potential benefits and broader impact of your work?

The winning move is to **stage the next steps** — name WHO must do WHAT for the benefit to
materialize — rather than promise a sweeping outcome. Impact you can defend beats impact you
assert.

Checklist:
- [ ] Names a specific beneficiary (a patient population, a field, a class of engineers, a follow-up study).
- [ ] States the concrete next step and WHO does it (you / a clinician / an engineer / a trial).
- [ ] Distinguishes what your result already establishes from what still needs doing.
- [ ] NO overclaim. Flag "revolutionize / transform / cure / disrupt" before results — a known red flag.
- [ ] Honest about scope and limits (consistent with the report's limitations).
- [ ] ≤ 200 words.

## Essay 3 — Your Potential as a Scientist / Mathematician / Engineer (200 words)

**Prompt intent.** What is your potential — your trajectory, your way of thinking, your drive?
This maps to "Promise as a Scientist" in the rubric.

The trap is turning it into a résumé recap (the activities and awards already live in Task 8).
Show ONE scene that reveals how you think — learning from a failure, an obsession, a moment of
insight — rather than listing accomplishments.

Checklist:
- [ ] Shows a way of thinking (analytical / persistent / curious), not a list of honors.
- [ ] One concrete scene or pattern, told in first person, that only this student could write.
- [ ] Connects to a forward trajectory (where this drive points), not just the past.
- [ ] Does NOT duplicate Task 8 (activities/awards) — adds the interior view those can't.
- [ ] Reflection / integrity moment is gold (rubric values learning from failure).
- [ ] ≤ 200 words.

## Essay 4 — Common App essay (choose ONE of three prompts; 350 words)

**Prompt intent.** A personal essay on one of three offered prompts. This is the place to
**think beyond the research project** — to show a distinctive identity or a genuine personal
stake. The exact three prompts can vary by cycle; read them off the current official questions
with the student rather than assuming. When the prompts are confirmed, help the student pick the
ONE that fits a story only they could tell.

The strongest exemplar (composite 19.33) tied the research topic to a personal stake — adopted
siblings who had faced food insecurity. That is **identity and stake**, not a list of
achievements. Aim for that register.

Choosing among the three prompts:
- Pick the prompt that unlocks a story **only this student** can tell.
- Reject any prompt that would become a polished résumé or a generic "I love science" essay.
- The best choice usually surfaces a value, a relationship, a turning point, or a tension — and
  shows the student's character through it.

Checklist:
- [ ] One of the three prompts chosen, and named, with a clear personal fit.
- [ ] A distinctive identity OR a genuine personal stake at the core.
- [ ] Goes BEYOND the research project (different lens than essays 1–3).
- [ ] Concrete, first-person, sensory — could-only-be-you. Two students could not both submit it.
- [ ] Not a résumé in paragraph form; not a generic passion statement.
- [ ] ≤ 350 words.

## Cross-essay consistency checks

- The same headline NUMBER appears in the paper, the layperson summary, and (where relevant) the
  impact essay — one figure, not three phrasings of it.
- The four essays do not repeat each other: summary = the science; impact = consequences; potential
  = how you think; Common App = who you are beyond the project.
- Voice is consistently the student's across all four. A sudden register change is an AI-flag risk.
- None of the four reads generic. Evaluators run AI-content detection and the Ethics Statement
  certifies the essays were not constructed with AI tools — concrete personal detail is the defense.
