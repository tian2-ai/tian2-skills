# Independence, disclosure, and the Project Recommendation

This file covers three linked things: how the mentor relationship affects the Student
Contribution score, what STS requires you to disclose (and that disclosure is mandatory even
though the underlying arrangement is permitted), and what a strong Project Recommendation
(Task 2b) actually contains. The mechanics of the forms live in `sts-rules-wizard`; the WRITING
of the Task 4 boxes lives in `sts-contribution-coach`. This file is the framing that makes both
of those land.

## Why independence is the product

Student Contribution is one of the four equally-weighted rubric criteria (25% of the total),
and Independence is its first sub-criterion. The rubric asks judges to separate student work
from mentor work, clearly and per-phase. Because the composite score is a SUM, a capped Student
Contribution caps the entire application — you cannot buy it back elsewhere.

From the verified OTT winner analysis, the floor every winner cleared was: the student did the
core work AND the mentor corroborated it specifically — letters that say things like "98% is
the student's own." The relationship has to be structured so that this sentence is TRUE and so
that the person closest to the work can say it.

## The honesty note you must tell the student

> A close-relative mentor, or a mentor who simply assigned the student a ready-made slice of an
> existing lab project that the student never extended, VISIBLY CAPS perceived independence —
> even when fully and correctly disclosed.

Disclosure keeps you compliant. It does not remove the ceiling that judges put on perceived
independence in these cases. Two practical consequences:

1. **Prefer an arms-length mentor** to a relative or a family connection. If a relative is the
   only option, that is allowed and must be disclosed — but the student should deliberately
   carve a clearly separable, student-owned core and document it, to push back on the ceiling.
2. **Prefer a self-originated question, or a visibly extended one**, over an assigned task.
   "I asked whether the lab's result also holds under condition Y, which they hadn't tested" is
   the kind of framing that reads as independence. Differentiator #5 in the winner analysis:
   own or visibly extend the question, and bound the mentor's role explicitly. A red flag that
   cost points: a close-relative or assigned mentor with no extension.

## What must be disclosed (permitted, but mandatory to disclose)

All three of these are allowed in STS. The risk is not the arrangement — it is FAILING TO
DISCLOSE it, which can disqualify the entry. Route the actual forms and wording to
`sts-rules-wizard`.

1. **Conflict of interest (COI)** — if the mentor is a relative, a family member, a family
   friend, or a parent's colleague, that is a conflict of interest and must be disclosed
   (Task 4 Q18 conflict-of-interest; STEM-family disclosure on Task 1; mentor relationship on
   Task 4 Q9). Disclosing does not penalize you for the relationship existing; hiding it can
   disqualify you.

2. **Payment disclosure** — STS requires disclosure of ALL payments for research or coaching.
   If the student or family paid for a research program, paid for lab access, paid a private
   coach, or paid for application coaching, that payment must be disclosed (Task 4 Q17
   additional support). Free, merit-based programs such as RSI involve no payment and nothing
   to disclose on this axis. Paid is permitted; undisclosed paid is a disqualification risk.

3. **AI-use disclosure** — separate axis, also routed to `sts-rules-wizard` (Task 4 Q15 and the
   Ethics Statement, which certifies the report and responses were NOT constructed with AI
   tools like ChatGPT). Mentioned here only so the student knows all three disclosures coexist.

## The Project Recommendation (Task 2b)

STS allows up to two Project Recommendations. They are confidential (the recommender submits
directly; the student never sees them) and they feed the Recommendations sub-criterion under
the Entry Form, which the rubric scores on specificity, insight, and comparison to peers.

### Who should write it

**The person closest to the research** — the mentor or PI who actually supervised the work,
not the most famous or most senior name in the building. A specific letter from the
postdoc/PI who watched the student debug the pipeline beats a generic letter from a department
chair who met the student twice. If two recommendations are allowed, the second can add a
different vantage point (e.g., a teacher), but at least one should be from whoever was closest
to the bench/keyboard.

### What a "Top 1%" letter contains

The strongest letters do three things:

1. **Rank explicitly, with a stated pool.** "The strongest high-school researcher I have
   mentored in fifteen years," "Top 1% of students I have worked with." A rank without a
   comparison pool is weaker than a rank with one.

2. **Corroborate independence with SPECIFIC, phase-by-phase anecdotes.** Generic praise
   ("hardworking and bright") is a red flag the winner analysis explicitly flags as thin. The
   strong letter narrates concrete moments mapped to the project's phases:
   - Purpose: "She proposed the central question herself after reading our 2024 paper."
   - Design: "He designed the control condition; I had not suggested it."
   - Implementation: "She built and debugged the analysis pipeline alone over winter break."
   - Data/analysis: "He chose the statistical test and caught an error in my own assumption."
   - Conclusions: "Roughly 98% of the analysis and the conclusions are the student's own work."
   The "98% is the student's own" type of corroboration is exactly the floor signal winners
   cleared. It only works if it is true.

3. **Reflect failure-response and integrity, not just competence.** Insight is a rubric
   sub-criterion: a letter that describes how the student handled a result that didn't work,
   or owned a mistake, signals the scientist's temperament judges reward.

### How to help the recommender write specifically — WITHOUT writing the letter

This is the line the student must not cross. The student gives the recommender raw material;
the recommender writes the letter in their own words. The student NEVER drafts or ghost-writes
the recommendation — doing so is dishonest and is a disqualification risk.

What the student provides — a "brag-sheet," phase by phase:

- A one-page bullet list of what the student actually did, organized by the six project phases
  (purpose, design, implementation, data gathering, analysis, conclusions).
- Concrete moments with dates: "Nov 12 — proposed the control condition in our meeting";
  "over winter break — rewrote the pipeline alone after the first version failed."
- The specific things the student owned vs. what the mentor provided, stated honestly.
- A reminder of the deadline and the submission link, plus a thank-you and plenty of lead time.

What the student does NOT do:

- Write sample text for the recommender to paste.
- Tell the recommender what rank to assign or what percentage to claim.
- Inflate or invent any anecdote. If a phase was mostly the mentor's, the brag-sheet says so;
  the letter will be more credible for it, and the Task 4 boxes must match.

The brag-sheet doubles as the raw material for the six Task 4 contribution boxes — so the
student's own responses and the recommender's letter corroborate each other honestly. Hand the
Task 4 writing to `sts-contribution-coach`.

## Quick decision aid

| Situation | Compliance | Independence ceiling | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arms-length PI, student-originated question | Standard disclosure | High ceiling | Document phase ownership; brief recommender |
| Arms-length PI, assigned task, student extended it | Standard disclosure | Good, if extension is visible | Make the extension explicit in Task 4 and the letter |
| Arms-length PI, assigned task, no extension | Standard disclosure | Capped (red flag) | Extend the question now, or own a sub-analysis |
| Relative / family-connection mentor | COI MUST be disclosed | Capped even when disclosed | Disclose; carve a clearly separable student core; prefer arms-length if possible |
| Paid program / paid coaching | Payment MUST be disclosed | Neutral if work is the student's | Disclose payment (Q17); independence still earned by the work |
