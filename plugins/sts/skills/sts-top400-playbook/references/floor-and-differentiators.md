# The FLOOR × DIFFERENTIATORS model — what separated STS winners

Derived from reading 11 On-The-Table (OTT) STS winners ranked #23 through #302 (mostly Physics)
plus the 19.33 calibration exemplar, checked against rubric.json and the OTT mechanics. Do not
invent numbers or patterns beyond this file and the cited source files (see SKILL.md provenance).

## The mental model in one line

The composite is a **SUM of four equally-weighted (25%) criteria**, not a max. One weak criterion
caps the whole composite. So: **clear the FLOOR on all four criteria FIRST; THEN stack
DIFFERENTIATORS.** A project that clears all four floors lands around the 16.5 raw region (near the
~16.375 raw cutoff at rank 350). Differentiators are what push a floor-clearing project from ~16.5
toward 18+.

```
composite = ⌊floor on EntryForm⌋ + ⌊floor on Merit⌋ + ⌊floor on Contribution⌋ + ⌊floor on Potential⌋
            then + differentiators (cross-validate / new / null / external stamp / own-the-question / quantify)
```

---

## THE FLOOR — the four non-negotiable gates (one per 25% criterion)

Every winner cleared ALL FOUR. Missing any one caps the composite no matter how strong the others.

### 1. Entry Form floor — a strong, sustained academic baseline
- Winners clustered at **SAT 1510–1600 (most ≥1550) / ACT 35–36**, **6–8 AP 5s**, real college-level
  coursework, and **at least one founded or led activity** (sustained commitment, not a résumé list).
- Test scores are OPTIONAL for STS and their absence is not penalized — but when present they are
  part of this criterion, and the academic record (transcript rigor, honors) always is.
- **Judge relative to opportunity.** The rubric explicitly demands resource-context and bias
  awareness (school resources / SES). A student from a low-resource school is assessed on what they
  did with what they had — do not treat the elite-school baseline as a hard requirement, treat it
  as the observed cluster.

### 2. Scientific Merit floor — a graduate-level method, executed well, with results
- A real graduate-level technique: PDE solver, DFT, Monte-Carlo, genuine inferential statistics, a
  trained/validated model — **never a science-fair demo**.
- Executed at **4–5/5 sophistication** on a real research question that produced results.
- A literature review or a plan without results is INELIGIBLE — it cannot clear this floor.

### 3. Student Contribution floor — student did the core work AND the mentor corroborates it
- The student performed the core intellectual work, and a mentor can corroborate it **specifically**
  ("≈98% is the student's own"), not with a generic endorsement.
- The mentor's role is **bounded and stated** (what the mentor provided vs what the student did),
  consistent with the six per-phase contribution boxes in Task 4.

### 4. Scientific Potential floor — an accessible hook + a distinctive identity
- An **accessible layperson hook**: one plain-English sentence a non-expert understands and cares
  about (drives the Layperson's Summary, Task 7).
- A **distinctive identity essay** — a real, specific voice about who they are as a scientist, not a
  template "I love science" essay.

> Diagnosis rule: the lowest floor gate is the binding constraint. ALWAYS fix the weakest floor
> gate before recommending any differentiator. Polishing a strength while a floor is unmet is wasted
> effort because the sum is capped by the weak criterion.

## THE SIX DIFFERENTIATORS — what moved 16.5 toward 18+

Pick the 2–3 a given project can realistically deliver. Do not recommend all six.

1. **Cross-validate every key claim with a second independent method.** The strongest rigor signal
   observed. (Execute via `/sts-data-analysis-tutor`.)
2. **Contribute something NEW** — a new term, a new method, a generalization — not just running an
   existing tool unchanged. (Frame in the report + contribution boxes via
   `/sts-research-report-coach`, `/sts-contribution-coach`.)
3. **A rigorous NULL result wins** if framed as **shrinking the search space**, not as a failure.
   "We ruled out X with 95% confidence" is a contribution. (Frame via `/sts-data-analysis-tutor`.)
4. **Get an external stamp** — publication / first-authorship / top science fair / olympiad /
   preprint. An independent credential the judges can anchor on. (Disclose in Task 6/16.)
5. **Own or visibly extend the question, and bound the mentor's role explicitly.** Show the question
   was yours or that you pushed it beyond the mentor's starting point; state precisely what the
   mentor did. (Execute via `/sts-contribution-coach`; mentor framing via `/sts-mentor-finder`.)
6. **Quantify significance and repeat the number.** Put a concrete magnitude on the result and echo
   it in the abstract, the layperson summary, and the conclusions ("76% less power", "100→10 keV").
   (Execute via `/sts-essay-coach`, `/sts-research-report-coach`.)

## RED FLAGS that cost points (all observed in the winner set)

Call these out in any diagnosis and give the fix.

- **"Limitations: None / N/A"** — the single biggest avoidable mistake. The rubric rewards
  acknowledged limitations. ALWAYS name **2–4 honest limitations**. (Fix via
  `/sts-data-analysis-tutor`, `/sts-contribution-coach`.)
- **Confirmatory results dressed as discovery** — reproducing a known result and presenting it as
  novel. Be honest about what is confirmation vs new.
- **Thin data behind big claims** — a small/single-run dataset supporting a sweeping conclusion.
  Either bound the claim to the data or strengthen the data.
- **Close-relative or assigned-program mentor with no visible extension** — looks like inherited
  work. Counter with differentiator #5 (own/extend the question, bound the role).
- **Thin / generic recommendations** — recommenders who can't speak specifically about the student
  weaken the Recommendations sub-criterion. Ask early, give them specifics (route via
  `/sts-application-navigator` timeline).
- **Overclaiming impact** ("revolutionize", "cure") before results exist — undermines credibility.
  Match the impact claim to the evidence.

## THE NO-LAB PLAYBOOK — a laptop and public data can reach the top tier

Surface this whenever a student lacks wet-lab / RRI access.

- **Theory / computational topics** where a laptop is the whole instrument, plus **public datasets**
  (e.g., Chandra X-ray, MODIS satellite), plus **rigor** (validate the pipeline against a known
  case, test the assumptions, report the nulls), reach the top tier.
- **Proof:** two home-based winners and the 19.33 calibration exemplar did exactly this.
- A missing lab does not lower the ceiling — it changes which floor and differentiators you target:
  the Scientific Merit floor is met by computational sophistication; the strongest differentiators
  become #1 (cross-validate), #2 (contribute something new), #3 (a rigorous null), and #6
  (quantify). Independence (#5) is often EASIER to demonstrate without a lab supervisor.
- Route the doing to `/sts-topic-finder` (a computationally-tractable, public-data question) and
  `/sts-data-analysis-tutor` (pipeline validation + cross-validation + honest nulls).

## Per-criterion: what a 5/5 looks like vs the floor

Use this to show a student the gap between "cleared the floor" and "exceptional". Scoring anchors
(from rubric.json): 5.0 = exceptional/groundbreaking; 4.0–4.5 = strong; 3.0–3.5 = adequate;
2.0–2.5 = limited; 1.0–1.5 = weak. Typical composite 9–12; OTT raw cutoff ≈ 16.375 at rank 350.

| Criterion (25%) | The FLOOR (clears the gate) | What a 5/5 looks like (differentiated) |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry Form** | Strong record relative to opportunity; ≥1 founded/led activity | Top-percentile academics + a sustained, led commitment + an external stamp (top fair/olympiad/publication) + recommenders who compare you to the best peers they've seen |
| **Scientific Merit** | A graduate-level method, results, 4–5/5 sophistication, not a demo | A claim cross-validated by a second independent method; something NEW contributed; significance quantified; a rigorous null that shrinks the search space |
| **Student Contribution** | Student did the core work; mentor corroborates specifically; role bounded | The student visibly OWNED or extended the question; the origin-of-idea and independence statements make the boundary unmistakable; resourcefulness relative to available means |
| **Scientific Potential** | Accessible layperson hook + a distinctive identity essay | Communication that makes a hard result legible to a non-expert, a number repeated for impact, and a trajectory/voice that reads as a future scientific leader |

> Remember the variance: rank-by-Z and rank-by-raw correlate only r ≈ 0.18, and you are scored
> relative to your category pool (Z-normalization). Hard-graded categories (Chemistry, Physics,
> Biochem) have higher raw bars; soft-graded ones (Behavioral, Social) lower raw but can post the
> highest Z. Maximize the controllables; do not chase a number you can't see.
