# Conrad Video & Website

These are not decoration. **Storytelling & Professionalism (20%)** explicitly encompasses
"questions 1, 2 + video + entire submission and all attachments," and its sub-criteria include
**Credibility Boost** ("Do the video and website reinforce the team's expertise and
trustworthiness?"). Marketing's **Engagement Channels** sub-criterion is specifically about the website.

Roughly a quarter of the total score is touched by these two artifacts.

**You do not write scripts or website copy.** Structure, critique, and technical compliance only.

---

## Hard requirements — check these first, every time

### Video
- [ ] **3–5 minutes.** Hard cap 5:00. Over = a compliance problem, not a style note.
- [ ] Features a **model** of the innovation — sketch, 3D model, storyboard, wireframe, physical
      prototype, or other visual artifact. **Required**, not optional.
- [ ] Uploaded as **MP4 under 800 MB**
- [ ] **Direct YouTube or Vimeo link**, set to **public or unlisted** (not private)
- [ ] Focused on the innovation, its capabilities and technology

### Website
- [ ] **Direct link** that works — may be hidden from search engines, must not require login
- [ ] Tells the team's **story**: mission, vision, values
- [ ] Displays a **model** of the innovation with explanatory imagery
- [ ] Communicates **brand** — name, logo, colour scheme, voice, design

### The image consistency rule
Use the **same model** across the video, the website, and the submitted innovation image.
The Student Guide says this explicitly. Three different-looking models read as three different
projects and cost Polish & Consistency.

### The link test — do this in an incognito window, before the deadline

> **Greencrete submitted a Canva `/edit` URL.** A judge clicking it lands in an editor, not a
> website. This is a real, observed, entirely avoidable failure.

Test every link logged out, on a different device. Then have someone outside the team test them.

## The Innovation Video

### Structure that scores (3–5 min)

| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | **The problem, concretely** | One person, one situation, one cost. Not a montage of global crises. |
| 0:30–1:00 | **The team** | Who you are, why you. Brief. |
| 1:00–3:00 | **The innovation — showing the model** | The core. Show it working or show the model explaining it. Mechanism, not adjectives. |
| 3:00–4:00 | **Evidence and market** | What proves it works; who buys it. |
| 4:00–4:45 | **The ask and the vision** | What you need, where this goes. |

**The middle two minutes are the video.** Judges watch to "understand the innovation and evaluate
its potential." If a judge finishes your video unable to explain the mechanism, it failed
regardless of production value.

### What judges have explicitly criticised

Direct from the Chief Judge's Q&A, on a real submission:

> *"Many comments are too casual and would put me on guard as an investor. For example, the comment
> 'Yeah, this is like three-stage, this is like seven-stage or something, I don't know, I didn't
> count it' significantly lowers my belief that you'll champion this product and use investor
> resources well. It's OK to say 'I don't know' sometimes, yet an entrepreneur should research and
> test or know the technology well enough to show a high likelihood of successful implementation."*

And the structural advice given alongside it:

> *"Think about your write-up and presentation as storytelling with a beginning including
> highlights and a teaser, a middle with information and proof, and an end with a punchline promise
> or final reveal. This script can be planned and rehearsed to eliminate repetition and convince
> the audience while managing emotional intensity."*

**Therefore, non-negotiables:**
- **Rehearse.** Unscripted improvisation reads as unpreparedness.
- **Never say "I don't know" about your own technology** on camera.
- **Don't repeat yourself.** The same judge flagged a video for stating "airplane emissions are
  2.5% of global emissions" three times.
- Vary emotional intensity — don't be uniformly excited for five minutes.

### Production notes that actually matter

Judges are told **not** to over-reward high-production media, precisely so well-resourced teams
don't win on polish. So:

**Worth the effort:** clear audio (the single biggest quality lever — use any external mic),
steady framing, the model visible and well-lit, legible on-screen numbers, correct captions/spelling.

**Not worth the effort:** cinematic b-roll, drone shots, motion graphics packages, licensed music beds.

An honest, clear, rehearsed video of a cardboard model beats a slick video that never explains
the mechanism.

## The Website

### Required pages, minimum viable

1. **Home** — the one-sentence pitch (the finalist template), the innovation image, a clear
   value statement
2. **The Innovation** — how it works, with the model and explanatory imagery
3. **The Team** — photos, names, roles
4. **Impact / Why it matters** — the problem, quantified
5. **Contact**
6. Embed the **Innovation Video**

### What the two relevant sub-criteria are actually asking

- **Credibility Boost** (Storytelling): does this site make me believe these people can execute?
  → Consistency, working links, no lorem ipsum, no placeholder images, correct spelling.
- **Engagement Channels** (Marketing): "How effective is the website to demonstrate product value,
  tell a story and engage potential customers and/or buyers?"
  → Is it written **for a customer**, or for a teacher? This is the distinction most student
  sites miss. Write for the buyer identified in Q6.

### Platform

Wix, Canva Sites, Squarespace, Carrd, GitHub Pages, Google Sites — all fine. Real 2026 finalists
used ordinary free builders. **The platform is not scored; the published, working link is.**

Whatever you use: **publish it, then open the public URL in an incognito window.**

### Brand consistency

"Brand" is named explicitly in the requirements: name, logo, colour scheme, voice, design.
Check that the brief, video, website, and innovation image agree on: the innovation's name and
spelling, the colour palette, the model shown, and the one-sentence description.

Inconsistency here is a pure, unforced loss on Polish & Consistency.

## Pre-submission link audit

Run this the day before the deadline, not the hour before:

- [ ] Website opens in **incognito**, no login, no editor mode
- [ ] Every internal link and nav item works
- [ ] Video link opens in incognito and **plays**
- [ ] Video is **under 5:00** (check the actual runtime)
- [ ] MP4 uploaded and **under 800 MB**
- [ ] Video privacy is **public or unlisted**, never private
- [ ] Innovation image, video model, and website model are the **same model**
- [ ] Team photo is high quality and well lit (it goes on the public finalists page if you advance)
- [ ] Spelling correct **on screen** as well as spoken
- [ ] Someone outside the team has opened both links successfully
