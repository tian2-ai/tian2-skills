## 1. What the competition actually is

The Conrad Challenge is a **purpose-driven innovation and entrepreneurship competition**, not a science fair.
Run by **Space Center Houston**, presented by **Equinor**, named for Pete Conrad (Apollo 12, third person on the Moon). Founded 2005.

This distinction governs everything:

| | Science fair (ISEF/STS) | Conrad Challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | *Is the science sound?* | *Would a reasonable investor want to learn more?* |
| Unit of work | A study with results | A venture with a product |
| Rewarded | Rigor, methodology, novelty of finding | Innovation + defensibility + market + money |
| Deliverable | Paper / poster | Brief + video + website (+ live pitch) |
| Data required | Yes | **No.** Evidence required; experiments optional |
| Team | Usually solo | **Mandatory 2–5 students** |

A team that writes a Conrad brief like an ISEF abstract will score 2s. The rubric asks
business questions, and 50% of the score (Marketing 20% + Finances 10% + half of Storytelling)
is business reasoning.

**Scale:** 2025–26 season had **1,500+ projects from 70+ countries**. 2024–25 had 2,000+ teams.
Roughly **3% become finalists** (~25–31 teams reach the Summit).

## 2. Eligibility and team rules

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Age | **13–18** at time of participation |
| Team size | **2–5 students** (never 1) |
| Coach | **Exactly one, age 18+** — teacher, parent, subject-matter expert, or other qualified mentor |
| Team changes | Members may be **removed** but **no new members added** after Activation Stage is submitted |
| Minimum | Team must always have ≥2 members |
| Repeat entrants | Allowed, **but** resubmitting a prior innovation requires "significant advancements or changes" |
| Geography | Anywhere in the world; teammates may span schools/cities/countries |

**Age is not a scoring category.** Judges are not told ages and score all teams to one standard.
13-year-olds have won. (Chief Judge cites a team of 13–14-year-olds who made finals and won awards.)

## 3. The three stages

### Stage 1 — Activation (free)
**2025–26 window:** Aug 28 – Oct 30, 2025, 11:59 PM ET
Register team + coach under one account in the Conrad Portal, agree to Rules & Terms,
name the innovation, pick a category, submit the Lean Canvas.

### Stage 2 — Innovation (paid, the real filter)
**2025–26 deadline:** Jan 8, 2026, 11:59 PM ET
Submit: Innovation Brief + Innovation Video + Website + team photo + innovation image + references PDF.
**Entry fee $499 USD per team.** Financial aid available on demonstrated need.
Judged by external expert judges. Coaching comments + Finalist announcements released **February**.

### Stage 3 — Innovation Summit (finalists only)
**2025–26 dates:** April 22–25, 2026, Space Center Houston + NASA Johnson Space Center, Houston, TX.
Power Pitch to expert judges, EXPO tabletop exhibit, workshops, tours.
**$499 per guest** to attend (financial aid available).
Top honor: **Pete Conrad Scholar** (one team per category).

### Timeline shape (any year)
```
Aug ──── Oct 30 ──────────── Jan 8 ────── Feb ──────── Apr 22-25
 │         │                   │           │              │
reg     Lean Canvas      Innovation    Finalists      Summit
opens      due            Stage due    announced    Power Pitch
        [FREE]            [$499]                     [$499/guest]
        ~2 months        ~10 weeks
```

**The brutal structural fact:** the gap between Lean Canvas (Oct 30) and Innovation Stage (Jan 8)
is ~10 weeks spanning winter holidays and exams. Teams that treat Oct 30 as the finish line lose.

## 4. Categories

Four standing + one rotating special category:

1. **Aerospace & Aviation**
2. **Cyber-Technology & Security**
3. **Energy & Environment** — sponsored by Equinor
4. **Health & Nutrition**
5. **Special category (rotates annually)** — 2024-25 and 2025-26: **The Water Challenge**
   ("Currents of Change"): water equity, efficiency, purity.

Category is chosen at Lean Canvas time and shapes which judges see you.

**Category boundaries are soft and gameable.** Real 2026 examples:
- *BeCure* — bee-mite detection by phone camera — competed in **Health & Nutrition**, not Energy & Environment.
- *Robo Therapy* — a tremor-suppressing exoskeleton — competed in **Cyber-Technology & Security**, not Health.
- *Echo/Pulse* — an EMG speech-restoration headset — competed in **Cyber-Technology**, not Health.

→ Strategic implication in `topic-engine/METHOD.md` §5: pick the category where your project is
*unusual*, not the one where it's obvious. See "category arbitrage."

## 5. The China Chapter (structurally important)

Conrad China (康莱德创新者大会中国站) launched **2020** under authorization from the global committee.
2026–27 is its **seventh season**.

- Runs its **own selection process and its own awards**, with a China regional final (typically March).
- Has **dedicated finalist slots** at the global Summit — the 2026 finalist roster lists a separate
  **"China Chapter Finalists"** section (B-ASE, DiveGuard, OctoScope, VisionBridge, Soaring Seeds).
- Own timeline: 2026–27 regular registration deadline **Dec 14, 2026**.
- Runs offline Lean Canvas workshops in Chinese schools.

**Practical consequence:** a China-based team chooses which pipeline to enter. The China Chapter is a
*second, parallel path to Houston* with a different competitive pool. This materially changes odds and
should be an explicit strategic decision, not a default.

## 6. Submission artifacts — exact specifications

### 6a. Lean Canvas — 12 questions, ~40 words each

| # | Question | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem** — customer need; social/environmental challenge | 40 w |
| 2 | **Existing Alternatives** — how is this solved today? | 40 w |
| 3 | **Solution** — key characteristics of the innovation | 40 w |
| 4 | **Key Metrics** — numbers that track success | 40 w |
| 5 | **Unique Value Proposition** — what makes it different | 40 w |
| 6 | **High Level Concept** — the tagline | **10 w** |
| 7 | **Sustainable Advantage** — why hard to copy | 40 w |
| 8 | **Channels** — how sold and delivered | 40 w |
| 9 | **Customer Segments** — target audience | 40 w |
| 10 | **Early Adopters** — the very first customers | 40 w |
| 11 | **Cost Structure** — most significant costs¹ | 40 w |
| 12 | **Revenue Streams** — how it makes money ongoing | 40 w |

¹ The 2025-26 Student Guide has a **typo**: items 10 and 11 are both printed "Early Adopters".
Item 11's body text ("What are the most significant costs?") makes clear it is **Cost Structure**.
Answer it as Cost Structure.

Lean Canvas is reviewed for **completeness and feasibility**, not scored competitively. It is a gate, not a contest.
Submissions are approved on a **rolling basis** — submit early, advance early, and buy weeks of Innovation Stage runway.

### 6b. Innovation Brief — 10 questions, 3,000 words total

**The single most important submission item.** Per-question limits:

| # | Question | Words | Feeds rubric theme |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Elevator Pitch** — essence, impact, customers, business potential | 150 | Storytelling + Innovation |
| 2 | **Team** — formation, roles, motivation, capabilities | 150 | Storytelling |
| 3 | **Opportunity** — what pain point does it address? | 300 | Innovation |
| 4 | **Innovation**² — design, technology, how it works, what's new/proprietary, impact qual+quant, **how IP is protected** | **750** | Innovation + Practicality |
| 5 | **Validation / Progress** — how validated, what progress | 450 | Practicality |
| 6 | **Market** — customers, segments, opportunity size, buyer≠payer, ecosystem | 300 | Marketing |
| 7 | **Competition** — what competes, comparison, advantages/disadvantages, positioning | 300 | Marketing |
| 8 | **Go-To-Market** — how to attract and sell, pilot customers, channel strategy | 150 | Marketing |
| 9 | **Business Model** — key revenues and costs, **price and cost of one unit** | 300 | Finances |
| 10 | **Fundraising** — funds needed, use of funds, cost to develop and roll out, sources | 150 | Finances |

² The 2025-26 guide labels Q4 "Key Metrics" but its body text is the Innovation question
(identical to the 2024-25 form's Q4 "Innovation"). Answer the **body text**, not the label.

**Plus:**
- **Reference Attachment** (PDF) — all sources cited in the brief. *Judges see and open this.*
- **Additional Attachments** — up to **2 optional** files providing visual context (diagrams,
  animations, CAD renders, test data). Under-used and high-leverage.

**Word budget insight:** Q4 alone is **750 words = 25% of the brief**, and it feeds *two* rubric
themes (Innovation 30% + Practicality 20% = 50% of total score). Q4 is where the competition
is won or lost. Most weak briefs spend Q4 on adjectives.

### 6c. Innovation Video
- **3–5 minutes**, hard cap 5:00
- Must **feature a model** of the innovation: sketch, 3D model, storyboard, wireframe, physical prototype, or other visual artifact
- Upload **both** a YouTube/Vimeo link (public or unlisted — must be accessible) **and** an MP4 **< 800 MB**
- Focus on the innovation, its capabilities and technology

### 6d. Website
- Tells the team's story (mission, vision, values)
- Displays a **model** of the innovation with explanatory imagery
- Communicates **brand** — name, logo, colour scheme, voice, design
- Accessible by direct link; may be hidden from search engines

### 6e. Team Photo + Innovation Image
High-quality. If you become a finalist these appear on the public Global Finalists page.
The innovation image should be **the same model** used in the video and website.

## 7. The scoring rubric

Five themes, weighted, each scored **1–5 in whole points only** (no 3.5), auto-weighted to a 100-point scale.

| Theme | Weight | Core question |
|---|---:|---|
| **Innovation** | **30%** | How new or unique? How impactful? |
| **Storytelling & Professionalism** | 20% | Would a reasonable investor be motivated to learn more? |
| **Practicality** | 20% | Will it work? |
| **Marketing Strategy** | 20% | Does the team understand key markets? |
| **Finances** | 10% | Does the team understand costs and funding? |

### The 1–5 scale
| | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | FAIR | Significant missing elements, poor communication |
| 2 | GOOD | Addresses requirements but incomplete, not developed enough to assess with confidence |
| 3 | VERY GOOD | Competent, worthy of further work, gives general confidence and interest |
| 4 | EXCELLENT | Convincing and thorough, strong internal logic, good technology or originality, attracted to work with or fund |
| 5 | EXEMPLARY | Role model performance, strong impact, original and awesome innovation, high confidence in rationale, evidence and storytelling |

**Most teams earn 2, 3 or 4 on each theme.** 5s are reserved for genuine wow.

### 2025–26 sub-criteria (25 total — the real checklist)

**Innovation (30%)**
- Originality — truly new or a novel combination? creative/scientific inspiration?
- Impact Potential — transforms an industry or delivers real benefit?
- Ambition Level — incremental, bold, or audacious?
- **Verification — has an online search confirmed uniqueness and ruled out duplicates?**
- **IP Defensibility — patent, trade secret, copyright, first mover, contracts, ecosystem capture?**

**Storytelling & Professionalism (20%)**
- Investor Appeal · Clarity of Opportunity · Credibility Boost (video+website) · Narrative Structure · Polish & Consistency

**Practicality (20%)**
- Feasibility · Realism (consistent with scientific principles) · Proof of Concept · Evidence Base · Next Steps

**Marketing Strategy (20%)**
- Market Insight · Entry & Adoption · Ecosystem Awareness · Differentiation · Engagement Channels (website)

**Finances (10%)**
- Cost Estimation · Revenue Projections (per unit **and** overall) · Funding Strategy · Budget Reasonableness · Financial Viability

### Finalist threshold
- No formal cutoff, but finalists **typically score above 85–90 points**.
- Finalists are usually a judge's **highest-scored** team.
- Each team is normally seen by **3+ judges**; a selection committee sees all scores.
- **Teams never see scores** — only the written comments.

## 8. Rules on originality, coaching, and AI — read this twice

These are the constraints that make or break a mentoring program.

### Originality
> "The team's idea must be original work that has been developed by the team as a group."

### Coach limits
> "Coaches **may guide** the students but **may not create any of the submitted elements** or
> provide **excessive influence on the concept design**."

### AI and citation
From the Rules & Regulations:
> Submissions "may not copy or use other materials — **including artificial intelligence (AI) tools** —
> without properly citing the source. Proper citation includes the standard information of a scientific
> citation, including authors, publication source, dates, volume, pages, or URL information."

From the Chief Judge's Q&A (more specific and more restrictive):
> "Student teams **self-certify** that they present original work with necessary attributions and
> **have not used AI for writing, programming, or key innovation**. AI must be used only for
> **partial, original submission writing**."
> "Teams may use AI for research, and AI **should be footnoted as any other source** or quoted
> directly with footnoting. Judges will see a **research attribution page (PDF)** on the submission page."

**Separately and explicitly permitted:** teams may build AI *into the innovation itself*. Judges then
evaluate whether the team can explain how the AI functions — "it's not convincing for teams to write
that AI will solve a problem or create action unexplainedly."

### The three-way distinction that everything depends on

| Use of AI | Status |
|---|---|
| AI **inside the product** you invented | ✅ Encouraged — but you must explain how it works |
| AI as a **research/learning tool** | ✅ Allowed — **must be cited** in the references PDF |
| AI **writing your brief, code, or key innovation** | ❌ Prohibited — you self-certify you didn't |

### Enforcement
- Suspected plagiarism/AI misuse → judges report to `experts@conradchallenge.org`.
- "We examine this more so for teams that show finalist potential." → **the scrutiny scales with success.**
- Space Center Houston may disqualify a team at any point, at sole discretion.

### IP ownership
Teams **retain ownership** of their intellectual property.

## 11. Judging operations (what actually happens to your submission)

- Judging window ≈ **Jan 18 – Feb 4**; judges commit 5–10 hours.
- Each judge gets **up to 5–6 teams**, ~1–2 hours per team.
- Judges work **independently**; they may not consult each other or contact students.
- Judges are **required to perform web research** to verify originality, technical veracity, and market fit.
- Judges must complete **all five comment fields** in prose — short phrases are explicitly insufficient.
- Judges are told to write **as coaches, not graders**, and to address students directly ("you", not "they").
- Judges are told to have **moderate expectations on Finances** and be more lenient there.
- A judge's comments can be **rejected/recused** by the Chief Judge for poor quality or errors.

**Implication for teams:** your reader is a domain professional who will Google your claims,
is time-boxed to ~90 minutes, is reading 5 teams, and is instructed to be kind in writing while
scoring honestly. Optimise for *fast verifiability*.
